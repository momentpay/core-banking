# ecb_loans: Loans: products, schedules, accounts and transactions

> **Phase** 4  |  **Status** in progress (16 Nov 2026). Built: the loan product tables and loan products (list, one, template, create) for the core term loan; the repayment schedule engine, a port of Fineract's cumulative schedule generators for flat and declining interest, equal installments and equal principal, grace periods, daily or per-period interest, partial periods and installment rounding, with `POST /loans?command=calculateLoanSchedule` on top; and loan applications: submit (`POST /loans`), read one (with `associations`), by external id and a page of loans, approve (for a smaller amount or another disbursement date, which works the schedule out again), undo approval, reject, withdraw by the applicant and delete an application, all replaying Fineract's answers. A request for anything beyond the core (charges, several disbursements, floating rates, interest recalculation, group loans, collateral, linked savings, a fixed EMI, interest charged from a later date, repayment dates moved by a non-working day or holiday) is refused with a plain "not supported yet", never ignored. Changing a loan product or an application, and the loan template, are not built yet. Disbursement of a loan (one payout, accounting off) is built, with its transactions (disbursement and accrual) and the loan summary. Repayments (`mifos-standard-strategy`: penalties, fees, interest, principal on each installment in turn; overpaid and closed loans) are built. Still to do: undoing and adjusting a repayment, waivers and write-offs, the journal entries (a product with accounting on cannot be paid out yet), and the paged transaction list.  |  **Depends on** `ecb_money`, `ecb_platform`, `ecb_org`, `ecb_party`, `ecb_catalog`, `ecb_ledger`, `ecb_deposits`, `ecb_collateral`

Generated parts of this document (the Fineract reference, API scope, data model and tests) come from
`parity/` inventories and are regenerated by `python parity/tools/module_docs.py build`. The rest is the
design and lives in `docs/modules/modules.json`. Overall approach: [design reference](../design/00-fineract-design-reference.md), [ADR-0001](../adr/0001-follow-fineract-design.md).

## 1. Purpose

The largest and highest-risk module: loan products, the repayment schedule generator, loan accounts and their whole lifecycle, transactions and the allocation of payments, charges, interest recalculation, reschedule and restructure, write-off and charge-off, delinquency, credit bureau, progressive loans and the newer working-capital loans. Fineract devotes about a third of its code to this area, so it is built around a pure schedule engine that can be tested exhaustively against the oracle.

## 2. Responsibilities

- Loan products (`m_product_loan` and its configurable attributes, recalculation, variable installments, borrower-cycle variations, guarantee settings)
- The repayment schedule engine: equal installments and equal principal, declining balance and flat interest, interest calculation periods, grace, moratorium, multi-disbursement, variable installments
- Loan accounts and lifecycle: submit, approve, disburse, undo, reject, withdraw, close, reschedule, re-age, re-amortise, contract termination
- Transactions: repayment, prepayment, waive, write-off, charge-off, chargeback, refund, credit balance refund, merchant-issued refund, interest payment waiver, down payment
- Payment allocation strategies (the loan transaction processor and advanced payment allocation)
- Charges on loans, capitalised income, buy-down fees, interest pause
- Delinquency buckets and ranges, with pausing
- Progressive loan schedule model and the loan origination details
- Credit bureau configuration and integration
- Working-capital loans: products, accounts, amortisation, breach and near-breach, delinquency, charges and charge-off

## 3. Out of scope

- Collateral and guarantors (ecb_collateral)
- Locks and close-of-business steps (ecb_batch)
- Origination workflow and scoring: the core stays core
- Group and JLG loans (deferred)

## 7. Design to follow

- A pure schedule engine: input is the product terms plus the application, output is the list of installments (`m_loan_repayment_schedule` carries principal, interest, fee and penalty amounts, each with completed, written-off and waived components). No database and no clock inside it; Fineract itself extracted an 'embeddable schedule generator' module for the progressive model, which confirms the direction
- Installment amounts are split by component and each component tracks `_completed_derived`, `_writtenoff_derived` and `_waived_derived`; keep these columns because payment allocation updates them
- Payment allocation is pluggable: the loan transaction processor strategies and the advanced payment allocation rules decide how a repayment is split across principal, interest, fees and penalties and across installments. Port the strategies as data-driven rules, not hard-coded branches
- Transactions are immutable with reversal and replay: a back-dated transaction triggers reprocessing of later transactions (reverse-replay). This is where most of Fineract's edge cases live
- 35 tables reference `m_loan`; the loan row is large and many features hang off it, so organise the Elixir code by feature context (charges, delinquency, interest pause, originators) rather than one giant module
- Working-capital loans are a separate model in Fineract (their own tables, `m_wc_*`, schedule, breach and delinquency rules); build them after the progressive model and consider a separate app (open question)
- Accrual and journal behaviour is per product accounting rule; every transaction type has a defined set of journal lines

## 8. Build order

1. Pure schedule engine for the common cases (declining balance and flat, equal installments), tested against the oracle
2. Loan products and the template endpoints
3. Loan application, approval, disbursement with the schedule persisted
4. Repayment with the default allocation strategy, then reversal and reverse-replay
5. Charges, waive, write-off, charge-off, chargeback, refunds, interest recalculation
6. Reschedule, re-age, re-amortise, contract termination, down payment
7. Delinquency, interest pause, credit bureau
8. Advanced payment allocation and the progressive model
9. Working-capital loans

## 10. Acceptance criteria

Module-specific:

- For a generated corpus of loan terms the schedule matches Fineract line by line, including rounding
- For generated lifecycles with back-dated payments, reversals and charge-offs, every transaction, balance and journal matches Fineract
- No unexplained diff across the whole corpus blocks the module
- The loan integration classes (about 180) pass, or each failure is a recorded divergence

Common to every module:

- Contract probe: every `must` read operation of this module passes against the oracle (status and response shape).
- Fineract's integration-test classes listed above pass, or each failure is recorded as a divergence with an ADR.
- Every state-changing operation goes through the command bus: validated, authorised, audited, idempotent, maker-checker aware.
- Every table has an Ecto migration in the tenant repo that matches the Fineract schema, and the cross-tenant isolation test covers the module.
- Money uses `Decimal` with an explicit currency and rounding rule; no floats.
- Dependencies point only at the modules in the header. `mix xref` finds no cycle or reverse dependency.
- `mix format`, Credo strict, Dialyzer and the module's tests pass; the module README and this document are updated.
